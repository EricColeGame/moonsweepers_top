Moon Sweepers is, at its core, a resource management loop dressed up as the world's most underfunded janitorial contract. You and up to 15 friends are sent by the Lunar Surface Department (LSD) to clean an entire Moon covered in dust — and your starting budget covers exactly one toothbrush. Everything else, from better tools to cleaning vehicles, has to be earned by collecting, hauling, and selling lunar dust.

This guide breaks down how that economy works based on the game's official details, so you know exactly where your time (and dust) should go.

## The Core Loop: Collect, Deliver, Get Paid

Resource management in Moon Sweepers revolves around a simple but satisfying cycle:

- **Collect lunar dust** using your current cleaning tool
- **Fill your storage** with everything you've gathered
- **Deliver it to a depot** and launch shipments
- **Get paid**, then reinvest those earnings

Every brushstroke matters, especially early on. With nothing but a toothbrush, your collection rate is painfully small, which makes each upgrade purchase feel like a genuine jump in productivity rather than a marginal tweak.

## Why Depots Matter

Depots aren't just drop-off points — they're a strategic resource decision. According to the official game description, you can **place depots near your work**, which directly affects how much time you spend hauling versus actually cleaning.

The trade-off is straightforward: the closer your depot is to your work zone, the shorter your delivery trips. Since you only get paid when you deliver dust and launch shipments, poor depot placement means long walks with a full storage tank and slower earnings. As your operation grows and you push into new territory, repositioning depots keeps your supply chain efficient.

## Upgrades: Tools and Carrying Capacity

Your earnings feed into two upgrade paths that shape how you play:

- **Better cleaning tools** — faster, more effective ways to strip dust off the terrain than a toothbrush
- **Increased carrying capacity** — bigger storage so you can take on bigger jobs between deliveries

Carrying capacity is the quieter of the two upgrades, but it's central to good resource management. More storage per trip means fewer depot runs, and fewer depot runs means more uninterrupted cleaning time. Balancing tool upgrades against capacity upgrades is the main strategic tension in the game.

## Scaling Up: Vehicles and Automation

As your operation grows, Moon Sweepers lets you **unlock cleaning vehicles** and put them to work. The game carries user tags for Automation, Base Building, and Incremental on Steam, and vehicles are where the shift happens — from personally scrubbing the surface to managing a broader cleaning operation.

Progress is also visible and persistent: the terrain turns clean with every pass, and you can track your progress across the lunar surface to see how much Moon you've actually covered.

## Managing Resources in Co-op

Moon Sweepers supports **online co-op lobbies of up to 16 players**, and that adds a human element to resource management. You can spread out to cover more ground or tackle the same area together — there's enough dust to keep everyone busy. The official description wryly notes that whether everyone actually works is another matter.

In practice, co-op changes the math: more players means more dust flowing into depots, and shared territory means depot placement decisions affect the whole crew.

## FAQ

**How does resource management work in Moon Sweepers?**
You collect lunar dust with your cleaning tools, fill your personal storage, deliver it to a depot, and launch shipments to earn money. Earnings buy better tools and increased carrying capacity, which speeds up the whole loop.

**Do you really start with a toothbrush?**
Yes — the official description says the LSD's budget only covered a toothbrush, and upgrading your tools from that humble start is a core part of the game's progression.

**How many players can play Moon Sweepers together?**
Online co-op supports up to 16 players in a single lobby, with the option to spread out across the surface or clean the same area together.

**Are depots worth placing near your work?**
The game explicitly lets you place depots near your work area, and since you only get paid by delivering dust to them, shorter delivery distances mean faster earnings — a smart depot network is key to efficient resource management.
