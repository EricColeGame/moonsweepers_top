Moon Sweepers starts you off with nothing but a toothbrush, and the upgrade system is what turns that humble tool into a full-scale cleanup operation. The problem is that the game offers several upgrade tracks at once — handheld tools, dust capacity, vehicles, automated cleaners, and the Upgrade Matrix — and buying everything in order is a good way to waste money. The core principle: upgrade whatever interrupts your cleaning most often, not whatever is newest on the shop screen.

## Where the upgrade system lives

Before planning a purchase order, know that upgrades are split across four locations:

- **Station supply area** — handheld tools and suit equipment. As of update 1.0.4, helmet lamp upgrades are found on the supply wall.
- **Upgrade Matrix** — broader cleaning and economy improvements. In co-op, your crew shares the host's matrix.
- **Garage** — recall, store, and upgrade vehicles you own.
- **Construction Tool** — unlocks structures and production machinery, which require space, connections, and sometimes power.

If an upgrade you saw in an older video has vanished, check *where* it's now sold before assuming your save is broken — the relocated helmet lamp upgrades are a good example.

## Escaping the toothbrush stage

The toothbrush will earn your first dust shipments, but its narrow cleaning path makes it a poor long-term tool. Your first real goal is a brush that clears noticeably more ground per pass. The early progression includes the Nail Brush and larger brooms like the Household Broom, Wide Broom, and Extra Wide Broom.

You don't need to buy and fully upgrade every step. If a wider tool is within reach, saving for it often beats spending repeatedly on a brush you'll soon shelve. Player-written launch guides describe both a careful Nail Brush route and a faster sprint toward wide brooms.

## Width or capacity: pick the one that's actually slowing you down

These two upgrades solve different problems. **Width** cuts the time spent clearing ground; **capacity** cuts how often a full container interrupts you. Run one ordinary cleaning session and watch what fails first:

- Too many strokes per patch? Prioritize a wider cleaning head.
- Container full before the patch is done? Prioritize capacity.
- Brush feels fine but you're wasting time crossing open ground? Start saving for a vehicle.

The same test applies to convenience upgrades like automatic packing. They remove an interruption, but they won't make a small brush cover more ground.

## Vehicles vs. automated cleaners: choosing your path

After a few brush upgrades, your biggest decision is *how* you want to clean:

- **Vehicle path** — suits players who enjoy driving across terrain and clearing broad stretches. The garage handles management and upgrades. Before spending heavily, check how you'll actually unload the vehicle's dust.
- **Robot vacuum path** — ideal for cleaning one defined area while you do something else. Placement matters enormously, since the robot only works on dusty ground it can reach, and you should know where its collected dust goes before buying more.

There's no universal winner. The right purchase is the one you can put to work immediately with a reliable dust-handling setup behind it.

## Which Upgrade Matrix choices are worth it?

Matrix upgrades are situational, not sequential:

- **Cleaning improvements** — priority while you sweep manually most of the time.
- **Market Contracts** — payout boosts make sense once you're regularly selling large shipments.
- **Region Bounty** — valuable when you consistently finish sections rather than leaving them half done.
- **Supplier Network** — buy it *before* a wave of expensive purchases; a discount bought afterward helps no one.

Note that patch 1.0.4 fixed a map display issue involving full section payments and the Region Bounty, so older screenshots may show incomplete payout information. For vehicles, upgrade the limitation you actually notice — coverage, storage, speed, or unloading time — and invest in the vehicle you drive, not a fleet you don't.

## Co-op: what "shared upgrades" really means

Update 1.0.3 changed the launch rule: everyone in a crew uses the **host's Upgrade Matrix**, and any player's matrix purchase benefits the whole crew. Older saves keep the highest level already bought. Crucially, that sharing applies to the *matrix only* — it doesn't give every teammate a copy of purchased tools or vehicles. Agree on spending before touching shared money. The same update also made owned vehicles manageable from any garage, so guides insisting you return to one specific garage may be describing an earlier build.

## A practical upgrade order

If you want a simple route:

1. Replace the toothbrush with a meaningfully wider brush.
2. Increase capacity once full containers become your main interruption.
3. Pick a direction — garage and vehicle, or a stationary cleaner for a defined area.
4. Add late-game robots and vehicle upgrades when your dust-handling setup can keep up.

Reassess after each major purchase. The best upgrade in Moon Sweepers is the one that removes the slowdown you can see in an ordinary session — not the priciest item on the shelf.

## FAQ

**What should I buy first in Moon Sweepers?**
A better brush. Get out of the toothbrush stage with a tool that clears more ground per pass, then add dust capacity when full containers start interrupting you.

**How does the Moon Sweepers upgrade system work in co-op?**
Since update 1.0.3, the crew shares the host's Upgrade Matrix — any player's matrix purchase upgrades everyone. Tools and vehicles, however, are not duplicated for each teammate.

**Should I buy a vehicle or a robot vacuum?**
Choose a vehicle if you want to clean large areas yourself; choose a stationary robot if you want one spot cleaned while you focus elsewhere. Whichever you pick, plan how its collected dust gets sold.

**Where did the helmet lamp upgrade go?**
As of update 1.0.4, helmet lamp upgrades moved to the station's supply wall. If an upgrade seems missing, check its new location before troubleshooting your save.
