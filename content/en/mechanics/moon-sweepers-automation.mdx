Moon Sweepers starts you off with a toothbrush and an entire moon covered in dust, but the game's real hook is automation. Once you unlock building tools, you can replace the trip-home-and-sell loop with conveyor belts, dust processing plants, and autonomous robot vacuums. Here's how the automation mechanics work and why they change the game entirely.

## The Grind Before Automation

When you first land, the moon is broken into hexagonal tiles, each thick with dust. Your starting toothbrush carves only a thin groove, and your backpack fills up fast. When it's full, you must walk back to base and sell the dust — each kilo nets you a single dollar. Those earnings buy better tools: a hand brush, then a broom, plus upgrades like wider brush heads and larger backpacks so you can stay in the field longer before cashing in.

## Your First Automation Purchase: The Hammer

The gateway to Moon Sweepers automation is a simple item: the hammer, sold at your base's shop. Once you own it, you unlock the ability to build the game's core industrial structures:

- **Conveyor belts** — haul dust for you
- **Space dust processing plants** — refine raw dust into pricier goods
- **Robot vacuums** — clean tiles without you lifting a finger

## How Conveyor Belts Work

Stretch a conveyor belt from your base out to whatever sector you're currently clearing. Instead of hauling full dust canisters home yourself, you dump them on the belt, and it whisks them back to base and sells them automatically. The practical benefit is huge: every trip home is time you're not spending scrubbing, so a well-placed belt keeps you brushing for far longer stretches.

## Processing Plants and Robot Vacuums

Raw dust sells for a dollar per kilo, but a processing plant compacts that dust into a more valuable resource, boosting the payout on the same labor. Meanwhile, robot vacuums — which look like NASA-branded Roombas — clear dust across a wide grid around their docking station, working through sectors entirely on their own while you expand elsewhere.

Between belts, refineries, autonomous vacuums, and moon rovers, the game shifts from an incremental cleaning loop into something closer to a factory-builder in the mold of Satisfactory. As Rock Paper Shotgun put it in their recent coverage of the game, it's "part idle game, part automation game."

## How Long Does Cleaning the Moon Actually Take?

Here's the sobering part: the in-game handbook lists **163,842 sectors** in total. Steam players report the scale bluntly — one reviewer, Marten, wrote that after 10 hours they'd maxed out all the latest tools and vehicles yet weren't even 1% done; another, Bim Fimberdim, hit 1% of the surface cleared at 20 hours; and Amigos_Of_Bako reported clearing 1.6% after 26 hours. Automation doesn't shorten the journey so much as it makes the mountain feel climbable — which is exactly the incremental-genre trick the game is built around.

## FAQ

**How do you unlock automation in Moon Sweepers?**
Buy the hammer from your base's shop. That one purchase unlocks building conveyor belts, dust processing plants, and robot vacuums.

**What does the conveyor belt do in Moon Sweepers?**
It automatically transports your filled dust canisters from the field back to base and sells them, eliminating the manual return trip so you can keep cleaning.

**Are robot vacuums worth it in Moon Sweepers?**
They clear dust across a wide grid around their docking station without any input from you, letting you expand your cleaned territory while your machines handle finished sectors.

**How many sectors are there on the moon?**
According to the in-game handbook, there are 163,842 sectors in total — a scale that players report takes far beyond even the maxed-out toolset to meaningfully chip away at.
