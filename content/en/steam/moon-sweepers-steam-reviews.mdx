## Moon Sweepers Steam Reviews: The Verdict So Far

Moon Sweepers has an unusually split reception: players consistently love the first several hours, then hit a wall. The most helpful reviews on the game's [Steam Community page](https://steamcommunity.com/app/5335510/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated) — collected as of October 29, 2026 — skew toward "Not Recommended," even from players with 30+ hours logged. The core complaint isn't the concept, which reviewers repeatedly call fun and cool. It's that content, upgrades, and meaningful progression run out long before the moon is anywhere close to clean.

Here's what the review section actually says, positive and negative, so you can decide whether the early-game fun justifies the grind.

## What Players Love: A Strong, Satisfying Opening

Even the harshest critics acknowledge that Moon Sweepers starts well. Reviewers describe the early game as genuinely fun, with a satisfying loop of sweeping moon dust, upgrading your tools, and expanding your operation. The cooperative mode gets specific praise — one positive reviewer noted it's a blast with friends, and another highlighted a developer patch that fixed a multiplayer balance issue where upgrades were separate per player. According to that reviewer (posted October 24), host upgrades now apply to everyone in the session, and a developer responded directly to the review.

Players also clearly enjoy the sense of scale: watching your automation grow from manual broom work to machine-assisted cleaning is the hook that keeps people invested for 30+ hours.

## The Progression Problem: Upgrades Run Out Before the Moon Does

This is the single most common complaint across the reviews, and the numbers cited are striking:

- One player with 33.6 hours played reported unlocking **every upgrade after clearing roughly 0.6% of the moon**, and had only reached 1.6% cleaned by hour 26.
- A co-op group said that after about **7 hours with two friends**, they had every upgrade and could only place more producer machines — while sitting below 1% cleaned.
- A reviewer with 16.1 hours described all mechanics (power, automation, cleaning) as shallow, noting the best broom and vehicle arrive before 0.1% completion, that the power mechanic affects only two buildings, and that the automation category contains just four buildings plus conveyors.
- Another player estimated that finishing the moon solo after the upgrade ceiling would take **50–150 hours of repetitive actions** with no new progression after roughly hour six.

The most-detailed negative review (37.4 hours, posted October 27) described reaching 16% cleaned with 260 "joonas" units, only to find that station ports were at maximum throughput. Dust was being collected faster than it could be sold, effectively capping cleaning speed regardless of further purchases — with nothing left to buy since around 10% cleaned.

## Technical Issues: Performance Drops and Broken Saves

Late-game stability is the second major theme. A positive review (37.5 hours) documented a speedrun attempt at a 100% clean that fell apart due to performance: the player went from a smooth 144 FPS early on to struggling at 10 FPS with drops to 3 FPS. Worse, the game eventually broke entirely — machines stopped producing and displayed "waiting for..." messages despite working fine beforehand. The reviewer speculated that JOONAS rockets may be rendered at a huge range, contributing to the lag.

A separate review reported buildings simply stopped outputting dust planet-wide, freezing progression with no clear cause or fix. Other recurring technical gripes include:

- Conveyor belts described as janky, with crashes attributed to them
- The game pausing when the window loses focus, with no toggle to disable it — a problem for an incremental-style game meant to run passively
- Machines that won't reliably snap or latch together when placing

## The AI-Content Controversy

One of the most-upvoted negative reviews (162 "helpful" votes, 8.7 hours) raises a different concern: the reviewer claims the game is heavily AI-generated, pointing to gibberish textures on the astronaut model, Z-fighting on the station model, item descriptions that read like raw AI prompts, and upgrade trees that include useless options (such as speeding up an inventory-emptying process that already takes a fraction of a second). The reviewer also criticizes the absence of an Early Access tag, arguing the game's unfinished state means it's unlikely to be completed, and notes it isn't flagged for AI usage either. This is one player's account — the developer's position on these claims isn't represented in the review material — but the review's popularity suggests it resonates with part of the player base.

## Community Wishlist: What Reviewers Want Changed

Reviewers didn't just complain; many offered concrete suggestions that reveal where the game's gaps are:

- Deeper automation upgrade paths and conveyor speed upgrades, or buildable selling ports
- Better stats: cash per minute, dust production tracking, and general production numbers
- A portable minimap or compass directions
- Separate power lines and batteries instead of bundling everything into solar panels
- Upgrades for JOONAS units and more machine variety
- Machine rotation after purchase, without re-clicking the shop
- Faster vehicles with less dust capacity, plus consumable short-term boosts

## Should You Buy Moon Sweepers?

Based on the review consensus: Moon Sweepers is worth it if you want roughly five to ten enjoyable hours of co-op chaos and incremental cleaning. It's a poor fit if you're expecting a long-haul automation or base-building game — the reviews indicate the content ceiling arrives well before the 1% cleaned mark, late-game performance degrades badly, and several players encountered progression-freezing bugs. Multiple reviewers explicitly said they'd revisit if the developers add a roadmap, more upgrade tiers, and optimization passes.

## FAQ

**What is Moon Sweepers about?**
Moon Sweepers is an incremental cleaning and automation game where you sweep dust off the moon using brushes, vehicles, conveyors, and automated machines, selling the dust to fund upgrades.

**Are Moon Sweepers Steam reviews positive or negative?**
The most helpful reviews skew negative. Even players with 30+ hours praise the fun early game but criticize the lack of late-game content, performance drops, and bugs.

**Does Moon Sweepers work well in multiplayer?**
Players report co-op is fun in the early hours, and a patch (reported October 24) made host upgrades shared with all players, fixing a prior balance complaint.

**How long does it take to finish Moon Sweepers?**
Per community reviews, upgrades run out around 5–7 hours of play, and one reviewer estimated a full solo moon clean could take 50–150 repetitive hours afterward. No verified full completion appears in the review material.
