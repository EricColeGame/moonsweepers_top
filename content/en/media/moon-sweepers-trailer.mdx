## The Moon Sweepers Trailer, Explained

If you've seen the **Moon Sweepers trailer** on Steam and wondered what this quirky co-op cleaning sim actually is, the short answer: it's a first-person incremental cleaning game where you scrub an entire Moon with — at least initially — a toothbrush. Developer DragDrop Games released the game on October 23, 2026, and the Steam page currently hosts a "General / Cinematic Teaser trailer" running just 46 seconds, alongside gameplay footage and 17 screenshots.

The teaser leans into the game's central joke, and the store description confirms the setup: the Lunar Surface Department (LSD) has sent your crew to clean the Moon's dust-covered surface, but the budget only covered a toothbrush. From that absurd starting point, the trailer's promise is one of gradual, satisfying escalation — better tools, bigger storage, and eventually cleaning vehicles.

## What the Trailer Shows and Where to Watch It

## The Game Behind the Trailer: Core Loop and Progression

The trailer's "one toothbrush" premise pays off in a straightforward incremental progression:

- **Collect lunar dust** with your starting tool, watching terrain visibly turn clean with each pass.
- **Deliver to a depot** and launch shipments to get paid.
- **Upgrade tools and carrying capacity** so each trip between deliveries covers more ground.
- **Unlock cleaning vehicles** and place depots near your work area as the operation grows.

The game also features base building and automation elements per its user tags, and it's playable solo or in online co-op lobbies of up to 16 players. The store description adds a wry caveat about whether your friends will actually help.

## System Requirements and Platforms

If the trailer sold you, the good news is that Moon Sweepers is lightweight. Minimum specs call for a GTX 1650 or RX 570, 8 GB of RAM, and just 2 GB of storage on Windows 10. Recommended specs step up to a GTX 1660 Super and 16 GB of RAM. The game supports single-player, online co-op, Steam Achievements, Steam Cloud, Family Sharing, full controller support, and 19 interface languages. Steam Deck compatibility is currently listed as unknown.

## Should You Watch the Trailer or Just Buy?

The 46-second teaser does its job — it sells the joke and the vibe — but the real hook is the gameplay loop shown in the companion videos: visible progress across a whole Moon, co-op chaos with up to 16 friends, and a relaxing, incremental grind that players are clearly enjoying.
