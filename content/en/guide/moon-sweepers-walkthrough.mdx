Moon Sweepers gives you one deceptively simple goal: clean every square inch of the Moon. The catch is that there are multiple ways to go about it, and choosing the wrong upgrades early can slow your progress to a crawl. This Moon Sweepers walkthrough breaks down the loop — clean, collect, deliver, upgrade — and shows you where to spend your first dollars so the money keeps rolling in. The core cycle is straightforward: sweep Lunar Dust off the surface, haul it to the depot, fulfill contracts, and reinvest the profits into better tools, suit upgrades, and eventually vehicles.

## How Moon Sweepers Works

You start your adventure on the Moon with the sole objective of completing contracts. Cleaning the lunar surface isn't charity — the Lunar Dust you collect can be sold for profit, and that profit funds every tool and upgrade you'll ever need.

Here's the gameplay loop in a nutshell:

- **Clean** the surface and remove Lunar Dust using your available tools
- **Collect** the dust you've cleaned and deliver it to the depot
- **Complete contracts** by delivering the required amount of Lunar Dust
- **Reinvest** your earnings into tools, upgrades, and vehicles

There's no single fixed way to clean the Moon, but the order in which you unlock things matters a lot in the early game.

## Cleaning the Moon in the Early Game

Your first task is to establish a base. Once that's done, start cleaning sectors closest to your base — at this stage you have no vehicles for getting around, so travel time is your biggest enemy.

You'll likely begin with nothing but a toothbrush, and honestly, that's fine. The smart early play is to clear as many sectors as possible with the toothbrush without spending a dime. While you can pick up the Nail Brush or Hand Brush, it's better to hold off until you've cleared at least 15 sectors.

The key principle: **don't buy every tool.** Each cleaning tool has its own cleaning capacity and carry capacity, but you should only purchase items that give you a distinct advantage. The standout recommendation is to jump from the Toothbrush straight to the Household Broom — it's a massive upgrade for collecting Lunar Dust at a relatively cheap price. After that, skip the Wide Broom and Extra-Wide Broom entirely and put your money into collection-rate upgrades instead.

## Best Upgrade Priorities

There are several upgrade categories to choose from, and prioritization is everything when funds are tight. Here's a breakdown of what each category offers:

**Containers**

| Upgrade | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Toothbrush | Starter cleaning tool |
| Nail Brush | Slightly improved clearing tool |
| Hand Brush | Much more efficient cleaning |
| Household Broom | First cheap broom — a big early upgrade |
| Wide Broom | Larger operational radius than the Household Broom |
| Dust Compactor | Raises carry capacity of all containers |
| Auto-Packer | Automates dust packaging, saving time |

**Cleaning**

| Upgrade | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Wide Heads | Improves tool cleaning width |
| Deep Heads | Improves cleaning depth |
| Long Reach | Extends tool operational length |
| Surge Heads | Amplifies the previous three upgrades |

**Suit**

| Upgrade | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Servo Stride | Bonus movement speed |
| Lunar Leap | Aerial boost |
| Helmet Lamp | Light source |
| Exo Frame | Well-rounded exterior frame |

**Economy**

| Upgrade | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Market Contracts | Increases payout from selling Lunar Dust |
| Supplier Network | Bonus payments for completing a sector |
| Region Bounty | Bonus payout when selling Lunar Dust |
| Lunar Exchange | Bonus launch-payout bonuses |

If you play long enough, you'll eventually own everything. Early on, though, one upgrade stands above the rest: **Market Contracts**. It improves the valuation of every contract you fulfill, which means more money per delivery — and more money means faster access to every other upgrade on this list.

## Cleaning with Vehicles

Once you're past the opening stages, you'll craft your garage, which costs **$200**. From there, vehicles open up, starting with the Push Sweeper at **$300** and topping out at the Mega Vacuum Rover for a steep **$6,000**.

Every vehicle serves the same purpose: cleaning the Moon more efficiently. Their higher speed lets you reach distant sectors quickly, and as of patch 1.0.3 you can also add vehicle upgrades to make them even more effective.

A word of caution from the source guide's author: don't rush into expensive vehicles. Starting with a couple of Push Sweepers does the basic job well, and something like the Mega Vacuum Rover is best saved for the end game when the investment actually pays for itself.

## Making Money in Moon Sweepers

Early on, selling raw Lunar Dust is your only option — so sell it. But once you've built up some capital, shift your focus to **Moon Sand** and **Moonstone**. Both are far more valuable resources produced from Lunar Dust, and they'll make you rich in short order.

Automation is also part of the late-game economy, but it's a strict endgame activity. Once you reach that stage, you'll have already mastered every basic and advanced concept in this walkthrough.

## FAQ

**What is the goal in Moon Sweepers?**
The goal is to clean every square inch of the Moon. You do this by completing contracts — cleaning Lunar Dust off the surface, delivering it to the depot, and earning money to fund better tools and upgrades.

**What's the best first upgrade in Moon Sweepers?**
Market Contracts is the recommended first economy upgrade, since it increases the payout on every contract you fulfill. On the tool side, the Household Broom is the best early purchase after your starter Toothbrush.

**Should I buy every cleaning tool?**
No. Each tool has its own cleaning and carry capacity, but many overlap. It's more efficient to buy only the tools that offer a distinct advantage, like the Household Broom, and spend the rest on collection and economy upgrades.

**When should I buy vehicles like the Mega Vacuum Rover?**
Vehicles become available after you build a garage for $200, but expensive machines like the $6,000 Mega Vacuum Rover are end-game purchases. Start with cheaper Push Sweepers and save the big spenders for later.
