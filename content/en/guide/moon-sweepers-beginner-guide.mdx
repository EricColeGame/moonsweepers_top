Moon Sweepers hands you a toothbrush and points you at the Moon — and that's honestly all you need to get started. The core loop is simple: clean Lunar Dust off the surface, deliver it to the depot, complete contracts, and reinvest the money into better tools and upgrades. The confusion comes from the sheer number of paths available, since there's no single correct way to sweep every square inch of the lunar surface.

This beginner guide breaks down the smartest early-game order of operations so you don't waste cash on tools you don't need yet.

## How the Core Loop Works

Your adventure begins on the Moon with one objective: complete contracts. Cleaning the Moon isn't charity — the Lunar Dust you collect is a sellable resource, and every contract you fulfill puts money in your pocket.

The gameplay cycle looks like this:

- Clean the surface to remove Lunar Dust using whatever tools you have
- Collect the dust and deliver it to the depot
- Complete contracts by delivering the required amount of Lunar Dust
- Spend your earnings on tools, upgrades, and eventually vehicles

There's no fixed route through the game, but prioritizing correctly in the first few hours makes everything afterward dramatically smoother.

## Establish a Base and Clean With Your Toothbrush

Your first real task is establishing a base. Once it's up, focus on cleaning sectors close to home — at this stage you have no vehicles for getting around and very few advanced tools.

You'll almost certainly start with nothing but the toothbrush, and that's fine. Resist the urge to spend money early. Clear as many sectors as you can with the starter brush before buying anything, ideally at least 15 sectors before picking up a better tool like the nail brush or hand brush.

Every cleaning tool has its own cleaning capacity and carry capacity, so buying everything available is a trap. Instead, prioritize tools that give a distinct advantage — the Household Broom is the big one, offering a massive jump in dust collection for a relatively cheap price.

## Which Upgrades to Buy First

Upgrades are split into categories, and early money should go toward the ones with the biggest impact rather than a full shopping spree.

**Containers** cover your cleaning and carrying tools, from the starter Toothbrush through Nail Brush, Hand Brush, Household Broom, and Wide Broom, plus the Dust Compactor (raises carry capacity) and Auto-Packer (automates dust packaging). After the Household Broom, skip the Wide and Extra-Wide Brooms for now and invest in collection-rate upgrades instead.

**Cleaning** upgrades modify your tools: Wide Heads improve width, Deep Heads improve cleaning depth, and Long Reach extends operational length, while Surge Heads amplify all three.

**Suit** upgrades improve mobility and comfort — Servo Stride for movement speed, Lunar Leap for aerial boost, Helmet Lamp for light, and Exo Frame for a well-rounded exterior.

**Economy** upgrades multiply your income. Market Contracts, Supplier Network, Region Bounty, and Lunar Exchange all boost payouts in different ways.

If you buy only one economy upgrade early, make it **Market Contracts**. It improves the valuation of every contract you fulfill, which snowballs into more money for more upgrades and tools.

## When to Buy Vehicles

Once you're past the opening stages, you can craft a garage for $200. That unlocks vehicles ranging from the Push Sweeper at $300 all the way up to the Mega Vacuum Rover at $6,000.

Every vehicle serves the same purpose: cleaning the Moon more efficiently. Their higher speed lets you reach distant areas quickly, and as of patch 1.0.3, vehicles can also accept plenty of upgrades of their own.

The Mega Vacuum Rover sounds tempting, but save it for the endgame. A couple of Push Sweepers handle the early-to-mid game perfectly well, which is how many players — including our referenced [complete Moon Sweepers walkthrough at Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/complete-moon-sweepers-walkthrough/) — approach their first garage setup.

## Making Money Beyond Lunar Dust

Early on, selling raw Lunar Dust is your only income option. Eventually, though, you'll want to move up the value chain: Moon Sand sells for more, and Moonstone is produced from Lunar Dust and is even more valuable. These refined resources are how you get rich quickly in the mid and late game.

Automation exists in the game but is strictly an endgame activity — by the time you unlock it, you'll have already mastered every basic and advanced concept this guide covers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What should I do first in Moon Sweepers?**
Establish your base, then clean nearby sectors using only your toothbrush. Aim to clear at least 15 sectors before spending money on new tools.

**Which upgrade should beginners buy first?**
The Household Broom is the best early tool purchase, and Market Contracts is the top economy upgrade — it increases the payout of every contract you complete.

**When should I buy vehicles?**
Craft the garage for $200 once you're past the early game, then start with Push Sweepers at $300. Save expensive rovers like the $6,000 Mega Vacuum Rover for the endgame.

**How do I make more money than selling Lunar Dust?**
Progress to selling Moon Sand and producing Moonstone, both of which are made from Lunar Dust and are far more valuable.
