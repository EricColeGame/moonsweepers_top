Moon Sweepers gives you one job: clean an entire Moon. The catch? Your employer, the Lunar Surface Department (LSD), only budgeted enough for a single toothbrush. Released October 23, 2026 by DragDrop Games, this co-op cleaning sim plays out as a steady loop of scrubbing, collecting, delivering, and upgrading — playable solo or with up to 16 players in an online lobby.

Here's how the core loop works and what to focus on as you grow from one tiny brushstroke to a full lunar operation.

## The Core Gameplay Loop

Moon Sweepers is built around a simple, satisfying cycle:

1. **Collect lunar dust.** You start with a toothbrush and sweep dusty terrain by hand. Every pass visibly turns dirty ground clean.
2. **Fill your storage.** Dust you collect goes into your personal carrying capacity.
3. **Deliver to a depot.** Haul your full storage to a depot and launch shipments to get paid.
4. **Reinvest your earnings.** Spend your payout on better cleaning tools, increased carrying capacity, and eventually cleaning vehicles.

That's the whole game in four steps — but the incremental progression is where it hooks you. Each upgrade lets you collect more dust per trip and take on bigger jobs between deliveries.

## Progression: From Toothbrush to Vehicles

The upgrade path is the heart of Moon Sweepers. Your starting toothbrush is intentionally, comically inadequate — the Steam page describes it as "one painfully small brushstroke" at a time. As earnings come in, you'll want to prioritize:

- **Better cleaning tools** — faster and more efficient than scrubbing by hand
- **Increased carrying capacity** — more dust per run means fewer trips to the depot
- **Cleaning vehicles** — unlocked as your operation grows, letting you cover far more ground
- **Depot placement** — you can place depots near your work area to cut down travel time

You can also track your cleaning progress across the lunar surface, so you always know how much Moon is left to sweep.

## Playing Solo vs. Co-op With Up to 16 Players

You can play Moon Sweepers entirely solo, but it's designed with online co-op in mind — lobbies support up to 16 players. There are two ways to divide the work:

- **Spread out** and cover more ground across the lunar surface
- **Tackle the same area together** for faster clearing

There's enough dust to keep everyone busy. Whether your friends actually work, as the developers cheekily note, is another matter entirely.

If you're hosting a group, the first-person format and relaxed pacing make it easy for newcomers to drop in — the game is tagged as playable at your own pace, with no quick time events.

## Tips for New Sweepers

Based on the verified systems described by the developer:

- **Upgrade carrying capacity early.** More storage per trip directly reduces time spent shuttling back and forth to depots.
- **Place depots near your work zone.** Cutting travel distance is one of the few levers you control, and it compounds across every delivery.
- **Don't neglect tools.** Better brushes speed up the collection rate, which is your bottleneck at the start.
- **Work as a team in co-op.** With 16 players, coordinated coverage of different areas beats everyone crowding the same spot.
