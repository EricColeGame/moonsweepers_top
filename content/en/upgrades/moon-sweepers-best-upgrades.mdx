Moon Sweepers starts you off with a toothbrush, and every minute you spend with the wrong tool is time not spent shipping dust. The best upgrade strategy is simple: identify what interrupts your cleaning most often and fix that first. In practice, that means a better brush, then capacity, then a decision between vehicles and automation.

This guide walks through the upgrade categories, which purchases pay off early, and how to sequence the Moon Sweepers best upgrades for your playstyle — including co-op specifics.

## Know Where Each Upgrade Lives

Before spending, separate the different purchase types in Moon Sweepers:

- **Handheld tools and suit equipment** — sold at the station's supply area. As of update 1.0.4, helmet lamp upgrades sit on the supply wall.
- **Upgrade Matrix** — broader improvements covering cleaning and economy. In co-op, the crew shares the host's matrix.
- **Garage** — recall, store, and upgrade your vehicles.
- **Building equipment** — the Construction Tool opens up structures and production machinery, which need space, connections, and sometimes power.

If an upgrade you saw in an older video has vanished, check *where* it's sold now before assuming your save is broken. The relocated helmet lamp upgrades are a good example.

## Leave the Toothbrush Behind

Your first goal is a brush that clears noticeably more ground per pass. The early tool progression runs through the **Nail Brush** and larger brooms like the **Household Broom**, **Wide Broom**, and **Extra Wide Broom**.

You don't need to buy and fully upgrade every step. If you're close to affording a wider tool, saving up often beats spending repeatedly on a brush you'll soon shelve.

Use this simple diagnostic while cleaning:

- **Too many strokes per patch?** Prioritize a wider cleaning head.
- **Container full before you finish?** Prioritize capacity.
- **Comfortable with your brush but walking too much?** Start saving for a vehicle.

That last point matters most long-term: even a fully upgraded handheld tool is still handheld. Vehicles take on the much larger cleaning jobs once your work spreads beyond the starting station.

## Brush Width vs. Dust Capacity

These solve different problems. **Width** cuts the time spent clearing ground; **capacity** cuts how often a full container interrupts you.

Run one ordinary cleaning session and note what happens first. If your container fills while you're still sweeping a tiny strip, capacity is your smoother pick. If you sweep big patches fast and spend the rest of your run hauling full containers, capacity is the clearer next buy. The same test applies to convenience upgrades like automatic packing — buy them because you keep hitting the problem they solve, not because a list says so.

## Vehicles or Automated Cleaners: Pick Your Path

After the first few brush upgrades, your biggest decision is *how* you want to clean:

- **Vehicles** suit players who enjoy driving across new terrain and clearing broad stretches. The garage handles upgrades — but check how you'll unload a vehicle before investing heavily in cleaning or storage upgrades for it.
- **Stationary robot vacuums** clean one area while you do something else. Placement matters: their output depends on the dusty ground they can reach, and you should know where collected dust goes before adding more.

There's no universal winner. The right purchase is the one you can put to work immediately, with a reliable dust-handling plan behind it.

## Getting the Most From the Upgrade Matrix

Matrix upgrades are situational, not sequential:

- **Cleaning improvements** — prioritize while you still sweep manually most of the time.
- **Market Contracts** — worth it once you regularly sell substantial shipments.
- **Region Bounty** — pays off when you consistently finish sections rather than leaving them half-done.
- **Supplier Network** — buy before a spending spree, not after; a discount you purchase too late does nothing.

Note that update 1.0.4 fixed a map display issue involving full section payments and the Region Bounty, so older screenshots may show incomplete payout info. For vehicles, upgrade the one you actually drive — coverage, storage, speed, or unloading time, whichever limitation you feel.

## Co-op: Shared Upgrades Explained

Update 1.0.3 changed a key rule: everyone in a crew uses the **host's Upgrade Matrix**, and any player's matrix purchase upgrades the whole crew. Older saves keep the highest level previously bought.

Crucially, this applies to the *matrix only* — it doesn't give every teammate a copy of purchased tools or vehicles. Agree before spending shared money on who needs equipment and which matrix upgrade benefits the group's current workflow. The same update also made owned vehicles manageable from every garage, so older guides insisting on one specific garage are outdated.

## Is Night Vision Worth Buying?

If darkness is making you miss patches or long sessions uncomfortable, the Night Vision Goggles are a solid quality-of-life purchase. If you're still scraping together your first practical cleaning tool, solve that first.

## A Practical Upgrade Order

1. Replace the toothbrush with a meaningfully wider brush.
2. Increase capacity when full containers become your main interruption.
3. Pick a direction: save for a garage and vehicle, or set up an automated cleaner in a defined area.
4. Add late-game robots and further vehicle upgrades once your dust-handling setup can keep up.

Reassess after every major purchase. The best next upgrade is the one that removes the slowdown you actually see during an ordinary session — not necessarily the priciest item on screen. ## FAQ

**What should I buy first in Moon Sweepers?**
A better brush. The starting toothbrush earns your first shipments, but its narrow cleaning area makes it a poor long-term choice. Move toward the Nail Brush or a wider broom as soon as you can afford it.

**Should I upgrade brush width or dust capacity first?**
Whichever problem hits first. Run one cleaning session: if you're clearing ground too slowly, buy width; if full containers keep interrupting you, buy capacity.

**Do shared upgrades in co-op give everyone tools?**
No. Update 1.0.3 made the crew share the host's Upgrade Matrix, but purchased tools and vehicles aren't duplicated for each player. Coordinate before spending shared money.

**Are vehicles better than robot vacuums in Moon Sweepers?**
Neither is universally better. Vehicles suit hands-on players covering large areas; robot vacuums handle a defined zone while you work elsewhere. Choose the one you can deploy and unload effectively right now.
