Moon Sweepers gives you one deceptively simple goal: clean every square inch of the Moon. But there are multiple ways to go about it, and choosing the wrong tools or upgrades early on can slow your progress to a crawl. This Moon Sweepers gameplay guide breaks down the core loop — cleaning, delivering, completing contracts, and reinvesting your earnings — so you can build a profitable operation without wasting money.

## How Moon Sweepers Works

The premise is refreshingly straightforward. You start on the Moon with the objective of completing contracts, and cleaning isn't charity work — the Lunar Dust you collect can be sold for profit. That money funds new tools and upgrades.

The core loop looks like this:

- **Clean** the surface to remove Lunar Dust using your available tools.
- **Collect** the dust and deliver it to the depot.
- **Complete contracts** by delivering the required amount of Lunar Dust.
- **Reinvest** your earnings into upgrades that make future cleaning faster.

There's no single "correct" way to clean the Moon, but smart prioritization makes a massive difference in how quickly your operation grows.

## Cleaning the Moon: Your Early Game Strategy

Your first task is establishing a base. Once that's done, start cleaning sectors closest to it — at this stage, you have no vehicles and limited tools. You'll likely begin with nothing but a toothbrush, and that's completely fine.

The key early-game tip: clean as many sectors as possible with the toothbrush before spending anything. While you can buy upgrades like the nail brush or hand brush, it's more efficient to hold off until you've cleared at least 15 sectors.

Every cleaning tool has its own cleaning capacity and carry capacity. Resist the urge to buy everything. Instead, jump straight from the Toothbrush to the **Household Broom** — it's a massive upgrade for collecting Lunar Dust at a relatively low cost. After that, skip the Wide Broom and Extra-Wide Broom and put your money into collection-rate upgrades instead.

## Upgrade Priorities

There are five upgrade categories in Moon Sweepers. Here's a breakdown of what each one offers:

| Category | Upgrades |
| --- | --- |
| **Containers** | Toothbrush (starter tool), Nail Brush, Hand Brush, Household Broom, Wide Broom, Dust Compactor (raises carry capacity), Auto-Packer (automates dust packaging) |
| **Cleaning** | Wide Heads (tool width), Deep Heads (cleaning depth), Long Reach (operational length), Surge Heads (amplifies all three) |
| **Suit** | Servo Stride (movement speed), Lunar Leap (aerial boost), Helmet Lamp (light source), Exo Frame (well-rounded exterior frame) |
| **Economy** | Market Contracts (higher dust payouts), Supplier Network (sector completion bonuses), Region Bounty (selling bonuses), Lunar Exchange (launch-payout bonuses) |

You can eventually unlock everything, but early on, prioritize what compounds your income. **Market Contracts** should be your first economy upgrade — it improves the valuation of every contract you fulfill. More money means more upgrades and tools, which means even more money. That snowball is how you win the early game.

## Cleaning with Vehicles

Once you're past the early stages, you'll craft your garage — an important addition that costs $200. From there, you can purchase vehicles ranging from the $300 Push Sweeper all the way up to the $6,000 Mega Vacuum Rover.

Vehicles all serve the same purpose: cleaning the Moon more efficiently. Their higher speed lets you travel between areas far more quickly, and as of patch 1.0.3, you can also add vehicle upgrades to make them even more effective.

A word of caution from hands-on experience reported by [Destructoid's complete Moon Sweepers walkthrough](https://www.destructoid.com/complete-moon-sweepers-walkthrough/): don't rush into expensive vehicles. The author started with a couple of Push Sweepers once funds allowed, and recommends saving premium rides like the Mega Vacuum Rover for the end game.

## Making Money: From Dust to Moonstone

Early on, selling raw Lunar Dust is your only income source — but it won't stay that way. Eventually, you'll sell Moon Sand and produce Moonstone, both of which are far more valuable resources refined from Lunar Dust. Shifting to these higher-tier products is how you get rich quickly in the mid-to-late game.

Automation is worth mentioning here, though it's a strict endgame activity. Once you reach that stage, you'll have already mastered the fundamentals covered in this guide, and you won't need a walkthrough to figure out the rest.

## FAQ

**What is the goal in Moon Sweepers?**
The goal is to clean every square inch of the Moon. You do this by removing Lunar Dust, delivering it to the depot, and completing contracts to earn money for tools, upgrades, and vehicles.

**What's the best early upgrade in Moon Sweepers?**
Go from the Toothbrush directly to the Household Broom, then unlock Market Contracts first among economy upgrades. It boosts the payout of every contract you complete, accelerating your entire progression.

**Should I buy every cleaning tool in Moon Sweepers?**
No. Each tool has its own cleaning and carry capacity, but buying everything wastes money. Skip the Wide Broom and Extra-Wide Broom and invest in collection-rate upgrades instead.

**When should I buy vehicles in Moon Sweepers?**
After building your garage ($200), start with affordable Push Sweepers ($300). Save expensive vehicles like the $6,000 Mega Vacuum Rover for the end game, when you can afford them without stalling your upgrade path.
